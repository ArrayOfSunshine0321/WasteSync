Waste Recycling Awareness Guide

Why Should I Care?

Every year, millions of tons of domestic waste pollute municipal streets, drains, and ecosystems. Individual waste management habits directly impact global environmental sustainability:

  • Conserves Natural Resources: Recycling paper saves forests, while recycling plastics reduces reliance on crude oil extraction.
  • Saves Energy: Manufacturing products from recycled materials uses significantly less energy than refining virgin raw materials.
  • Reduces Landfill Overload: Proper segregation keeps non-biodegradable items out of dumping facilities, preventing soil and groundwater contamination.
  • Supports UN SDGs: Responsible segregation supports UN Sustainable Development Goals 11 (Sustainable Cities) & 12 (Responsible Consumption).

1. How to recycle plastic waste at home?

Rinse out food containers, milk bags, and bottles before throwing them into the recycling bin. Separate hard plastics (like water bottles) from soft plastics (like plastic bags). You can also reuse plastic jars as storage containers or turn plastic bottles into small plant pots.

2. Segregating Wet vs. Dry Waste

Keep organic kitchen waste (fruit peels, left-over food, tea bags) in a green bin for composting. Dry waste such as paper, cardboard, metal tins, and packaging materials should go into a blue bin.

3. E-Waste Management

Electronic items like old batteries, wires, chargers, and discarded devices contain toxic metals. Collect them separately and deposit them at designated municipal e-waste collection points.
